Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

Using historical SEO performance signals to prioritize content pages for human review and potential refresh.

Author: Prachi Choudhary   |   Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring   |   Date: September 2026

Abstract

This project asks whether historical SEO performance signals can be used to identify content pages that deserve review for a potential refresh.

The analysis is framed at the client-content pair level, using February 2026 information as the feature window and March 2026 performance as the outcome window.

An interpretable Decision Tree approach is compared with a transparent rule-based opportunity score using the same evaluation design.

The analysis is intended to produce a ranked set of content items for editorial review rather than establish causality or predict Google's ranking algorithm.

The final output is a decision-support action queue containing priority scores, reason codes, and recommended review actions.

1. Problem Statement

Content teams cannot manually investigate every page with equal depth. The goal of this project is therefore to create a repeatable method for deciding which pages should receive attention first.

Decision supported:
Which content pages should a content team prioritize for human review and possible refresh?

The unit of analysis is a client-content pair. The output is a ranked priority score and action queue that an editor can use to guide further investigation.

A wrong prioritization can waste editorial resources, while failing to prioritize a page that warrants review can leave a potential content opportunity unattended.

Data safety

Future outcome information was deliberately excluded from the predictive feature set.

Label-derived fields such as trend_direction and trend_pct were excluded because they can encode information about performance movement.

Pseudonymous identifiers such as client_hash_id and content_hash_id are used for identification and grouping only, never as predictive features.

No client names, domains, URLs, private search queries, credentials, or raw private exports are included in this public paper.

3. Methodology

Baseline

A transparent rule-based opportunity score serves as the baseline. It prioritizes pages using historical signals associated with potential content-review opportunities.

  • Lower CTR receives greater priority.
  • Weaker average search position receives greater priority.
  • Observed impressions contribute to prioritization.

Machine-learning model

The model is an interpretable Decision Tree Classifier. Decision trees were selected because their decisions can be inspected and communicated relatively easily to editorial stakeholders.

Target

The outcome represents whether an eligible content item went dark during the March outcome window based on March search-performance availability and impressions.

4. Evaluation

Evaluation uses a client-grouped train/test split so that observations belonging to the same client are not split across training and testing data.

February information is used as the predictive window, while March information is reserved for evaluating the outcome.

5. Interpretation

The analysis combines multiple historical SEO signals to produce a repeatable content-review priority.

Low CTR can indicate that a page receives search visibility but captures relatively few clicks.

A weaker average search position can indicate that a page is not performing strongly in search results relative to other pages.

Impressions provide an indication of observed search visibility and help distinguish pages with different levels of search exposure.

These relationships should be interpreted as directional evidence from the dataset rather than causal effects.

6. Ranked Recommendations

The final output is intended to be a prioritized queue for human editorial review.

Signal Recommended action
Low CTR Review title and meta description.
Weak ranking Review content depth, relevance and internal linking.
Strong visibility with weak capture Review search-result presentation and metadata.
Multiple weak signals Prioritize for deeper editorial review.
Editorial principle: The ranking supports human decision-making. It does not automatically determine which pages should be changed.

7. Limitations & Honest Framing

  • The analysis is observational rather than experimental.
  • Associations between SEO signals and outcomes should not be interpreted as causal effects.
  • The model does not predict or reproduce Google's ranking algorithm.
  • A high priority score does not guarantee that refreshing a page will improve future performance.
  • Editorial review remains necessary before taking action.

The results are therefore best described as observed, measured, directional, and decision-support oriented.
